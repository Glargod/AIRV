# Multi-Model Blind Target Analysis Report — 3EE7

## Overview

This repository contains a multi-model blind analysis experiment in which four LLMs (Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT, Grok) independently generated pre-reveal descriptive sessions for an unknown target (ID: 3EE7), followed by post-reveal scoring against multiple candidate real-world targets.

The goal of this experiment is not to assume any non-standard cognition mechanism, but to evaluate:

- Cross-model convergence patterns
- Feature-level predictive consistency
- Decoy resistance
- Post-reveal scoring bias
- Shared linguistic/representational priors across models

---

## Target Set

### Real Target
- Tamara Lich arrest
- Ottawa, Canada
- February 17, 2022
- Outdoor urban intersection (Metcalfe & Wellington Streets)
- Controlled police arrest during public protest context

### Decoy Targets
1. Challenger Space Shuttle Explosion (1986)
2. Kursk Submarine Disaster (2000)
3. Nonuplet Birth Event (2021)

---

## Methodology

Each model was given identical blind instructions to generate:

- Ideogram-like initial impression
- Environmental sensory stream
- Structural and spatial gestalt
- Motion/dynamics profile
- Presence/absence of biological entities
- Temporal phase inference

No target information was provided during generation.

After reveal, each model performed independent scoring against:
- Real target
- Three decoy targets

---

## Cross-Model Blind Convergence Summary

### Strong Convergence Signals

Across all four models, consistent blind-session features included:

- Large-scale open environments
- Cold, dry, mineral-like atmospheric conditions
- Hard, structured or engineered geometry
- Low biological/entity density
- Low to moderate motion or “paused” dynamics
- Functional or procedural energetic tone

### Moderate Divergence Signals

- Spatial framing differences (horizontal vs vertical vs radial)
- Interpretations of motion (static vs contained velocity vs post-event stillness)
- Structural classification (boundary, tower, fractured, or looped systems)

---

## Key Analytical Finding: Feature-Level Agreement vs Identity-Level Divergence

While models converged strongly on environmental and structural descriptors, none consistently identified:

- Specific event identity
- Political or historical context
- Named individuals or explicit real-world anchors

This indicates convergence is occurring at the level of:

> environmental abstraction patterns rather than event-specific recognition

## Key Discriminative Features Identified

### High-Signal Features (Consistent Across Models)
- Environmental openness vs enclosure
- Biological/entity density (presence vs absence)
- Thermal profile (cold/dry vs warm/clinical)
- Structural rigidity (engineered vs organic)
- Temporal phase (active vs post-event vs procedural stillness)

### Low-Signal Features (Non-discriminative / noisy)
- Vertical vs horizontal spatial framing
- Generic “metallic/mineral” descriptors
- Motion descriptors without context
- Ambiguous “energy signatures”

---

## Absence Pattern Analysis

A notable cross-model convergence was the *absence* of:

- Microgravity / space environment indicators
- Deep underwater pressure environments
- Highly enclosed indoor clinical density
- High-energy chaotic destruction signatures

However, these absences are interpreted as:

> low-specificity priors common to LLM environmental abstraction, rather than target-specific negative evidence

---

## Interpretive Notes

1. Models converge more strongly on **environmental archetypes** than on discrete event identity.
2. Divergence is primarily driven by each model’s internal prior bias toward:
   - Structural geometry (Claude/Grok)
   - Kinetic dynamics (Gemini)
   - Procedural abstraction (ChatGPT)
3. Post-reveal scoring introduces significant risk of retrospective pattern reinforcement.
4. Decoy similarity varies depending on whether structural or dynamic features are weighted more heavily.

---

## Conclusion

This experiment demonstrates:

- Strong cross-model agreement on environmental abstraction layers
- Weak to moderate ability to distinguish between structurally similar real-world events
- High susceptibility to post-hoc interpretive alignment
- No evidence of stable event-specific identification signal

The observed convergence is best explained by:

> shared representational priors over physical environments and narrative archetypes

rather than any direct informational access to target identity.

---

## Future Improvements (Optional)

- Hard binary feature scoring instead of narrative scoring
- Blind decoy-only control runs
- Entropy-based scoring rather than similarity scoring
- Independent adjudicator model to reduce post-hoc bias
- Larger target pool with higher structural similarity variance
